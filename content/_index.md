My research is at the intersection of labor and innovation, aimed at understanding what impacts inventors and the innovation process. I've worked in three main areas: inventors and the invention process, how skills impact inequality, and economic complexity. I integrate a variety of statistical techniques including applied microeconomics and network theory to understand labor and innovation.

I am an Assistant Professor of Economics at Pace University in New York, NY, where I primarily teach quantitative methods in economics.

Previously, I was a post-doctoral fellow at Brandeis University with [Adam Jaffe](https://scholar.google.com/citations?hl=en&user=Sx4G9YgAAAAJ) and [Margie Lachman](https://www.brandeis.edu/psychology/lachman/), working on the inventor creative life cycle with patent data. I completed my PhD at [UNU-MERIT](https://www.merit.unu.edu/) (Maastricht University), where my doctoral advisors were [Bart Verspagen](http://verspagen.nl/), Neil Foster-McGregor, and [Cesar Hidalgo](http://www.chidalgo.com/) of the Collective Learning group at the MIT Media Lab. In 2016, I was a visiting student and research assistant at the MIT Media Lab.

Before academia, I worked at UNICEF on resource mobilization and research on accessibility to health care. I received my master's and bachelor's degrees in economics from The New School for Social Research in New York City.
